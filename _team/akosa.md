is a Master of Public Health Candidate at The George Washington University and holds a Bachelor of Arts in Public Health Studies & Spanish from The Johns Hopkins University. He is a native of Prince George’s County, MD by way of Anambra State, Nigeria. He is an Assistant Editor for Keywords for Black Louisiana, writing stories and making editorial contributions to the Spanish translation work for the project. Akosa's work as a public health professional centers on investigating where and how interventions can be put in place to repair and improve quality of life for individuals and communities. His Master's thesis on *The Miseducation of Black Boys: Engaging Reformative Approaches in Preventing and Treating PTSD in Black Men* incorporates his experience, vision, and evaluation of Black studies, urban health disparities, health policy, and youth development in theory and practice. He lives and works in Baltimore as a Community School Coordinator at Elev8 Baltimore, a community-facing educational advocacy non-profit organization. Akosa's advocacy work have been featured in the Baltimore Banner, NPR, and the Washington Post.
