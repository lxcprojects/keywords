The “Selected Bibliography” page contains a selection of recommended readings, visual media, and other resources that the Keywords for Black Louisiana team has used to conduct their research. It is our hope that these resources will be helpful to those interested in learning more about themes and subjects encountered in the documents. The readings are organized by subject groups that correspond with [current keywords](/keywords_descriptions/).

# Texts We Study  

### Health and Wellness 

*Charity Hospital, disability, health, illness, wellness*


Bankole, Katherine Kemi. *Slavery and Medicine: Enslavement and Medical Practices in Antebellum Louisiana*. New York: Garland Publishing, 1998. 

Din, Gilbert C. and John E. Harkins. *The New Orleans Cabildo: Colonial Louisiana's First City Government, 1769-1803*. Baton Rouge: Louisiana State University Press, 1996. 

Salvaggio, John E. *New Orleans' Charity Hospital: A Story of Physicians, Politics, and Poverty*. Baton Rouge: Louisiana State University Press, 1992. 

### Social Worlds of Death

*death, funeral, murder, violence, religion*


Brown, Vincent. *The Reaper's Garden: Death and Power in the World of Atlantic Slavery*. Cambridge: Harvard University Press, 2008. 

### Degrees of Freedom

*coartación, emancipation, free people of color, freedom, freedom papers, freedom suit, manumission, self-purchase, tenancy*


Hartman, Saidiya. *Scenes of Subjection: Terror, Slavery, and Self-Making in Nineteenth-Century America*. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1997.

Mitchell, Mary Niall. *Raising Freedom’s Child: Black Children and Visions of the Future after Slavery*. New York: New York University Press, 2010.

Scott, Rebecca J. and Jean M. Hébrard. *Freedom Papers: An Atlantic Odyssey in the Age of Emancipation*. Cambridge: Harvard University Press, 2014.

### Navigating and Resisting Enslavement

*cimarrón, collaboration, communication, conspiracy, fugitivity, marronage, mobility, relocation, resistance, rival geography, survival, transportation, truancy*


Holmes, Jack D.L. “The Abortive Slave Revolt at Pointe Coupée, Louisiana, 1795.” *Louisiana History* 11, no. 4 (1970): 341-62. [https://www.jstor.org/stable/4231151](https://www.jstor.org/stable/4231151).

Johnson, Jessica Marie. *Wicked Flesh: Black Women, Intimacy, and Freedom in the Atlantic World*. Philadelphia: University of Pennsylvania, 2020.

### Criminality and Policing

*abuse, crime and punishment, imprisonment, policing, seizure, violence, war*


Din, Gilbert C. *Spaniards, Planters, and Slaves: The Spanish Regulation of Slavery in Louisiana, 1763-1803*. College Station: Texas A&M University Press, 1999.

### Geography

*Africa, Caribbean, Middle Passage, rival geography*


McKittrick, Katherine. *Demonic Grounds: Black Women and the Cartographies of Struggle*. Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, 2006.

Pierce, Jason. “For Its Incorporation in Our Union: The Louisiana Territory and the Conundrum of Western Expansion.” in *Making the White Man’s West: Whiteness and the Creation of the American West*. Boulder: University of Colorado Press, 2016.

Smallwood, Stephanie. *Saltwater Slavery: A Middle Passage from Africa to the American Diaspora*. Cambridge: Harvard University Press, 2007.

### Racial Capitalism

*contract labor, debt, displacement,  estate, exchange, fungibility, hiring out, income, inheritance, la traverse, lease, maritime, Middle Passage, payment, rental, sale, slave auction, slave inventory, trade, valuation*


Camp, Stephanie M. *Closer to Freedom: Enslaved Women and Everyday Resistance in the Plantation South*. Chapel Hill: University of North Carolina, 2004.

Johnson, Rashauna. *Slavery’s Metropolis: Unfree Labor in New Orleans during the Age of Revolutions*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2016.

Johnson, Walter. “To Remake the World: Slavery, Racial Capitalism, and Justice.” *Boston Review*. February 20, 2018. [https://bostonreview.net/forum/walter-johnson-to-remake-the-world/](https://bostonreview.net/forum/walter-johnson-to-remake-the-world/).

Morgan, Jennifer L. *Laboring Women: Reproduction and Gender in New World Slavery*. Philadelphia: University of Pennsylvania Press, 2004. 

Robinson, Cedric. *Black Marxism: The Making of the Black Radical Tradition*. Chapel Hill: University of North Carolina Press, 1983. 

Smallwood, Stephanie. *Saltwater Slavery: A Middle Passage from Africa to the American Diaspora*. Cambridge: Harvard University Press, 2007.

### Social Development and Kinship
*family, fatherhood, girlhood, kinship, boyhood, childhood, womanhood, witness, love, care, marriage, motherhood, play*

Clark, Emily. *The Strange History of the American Quadroon: Free Women of Color in the Revolutionary Atlantic World*. Chapel Hill: University of North Carolina, 2013.

### Race

*Afro-Indigeneity, mixed-race, mestizaje, metissage, race*


Aubert, Guillaume. “‘The Blood of France’: Race and Purity of Blood in the French Atlantic World.” *The William and Mary Quarterly* 61, no. 3 (July 2004): 439-78. [https://www.jstor.org/stable/3491805](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3491805).

Darryl Barthé,  Andrew Jolivétte, and Rain Prud’homme-Cranford, eds. *Louisiana Creole Peoplehood: Afro-Indigeneity & Community*. Seattle: University of Washington, 2022.

Clark, Emily. *The Strange History of the American Quadroon: Free Women of Color in the Revolutionary Atlantic World*. Chapel Hill: University of North Carolina, 2013.

Garraway, Doris L. *The Libertine Colony: Creolization in the Early French Caribbean*. Durham: Duke University, 2005.

Gray, D. Ryan. *Uprooted: Race, Public Housing, and the Archaeology of Four Lost New Orleans Neighborhoods*. Tuscaloosa: University of Alabama, 2020.

Edward Baptist. *The Half Has Never Been Told: Slavery and the Making of American Capitalism*. New York: Basic Books, 2014.

Darryl Barthé,  Andrew Jolivétte, and Rain Prud’homme-Cranford, eds. *Louisiana Creole Peoplehood: Afro-Indigeneity & Community*. Seattle: University of Washington, 2022.

Bolton, Herbert Eugene, ed. and trans. *Athanase de Mézières and the Louisiana-Texas Frontier, 1768-1780*. Cleveland: The Arthur H. Clark Company, 1914.

Blackbird, Leila K. “‘It Has Always Been Customary to Make Slaves of Savages’: The Problem of Indian Slavery in Spanish Louisiana Revisited, 1769-1803,” *The William & Mary Quarterly* 80, no. 3 (July 2023): 525-58. https://doi.org/10.1353/wmq.2023.a903166. 

Ellis, Elizabeth. “The Natchez War Revisited: Violence, Multinational Settlements, and Indigenous Diplomacy in the Lower Mississippi Valley.” *The William & Mary Quarterly* 77, no. 3 (2020): 441-72. https://www.muse.jhu.edu/article/761429. 

Ellis, Elizabeth. *The Great Power of Small Nations: Indigenous Diplomacy in the Gulf South*. Philadelphia: University of Pennsylvania Press, 2023.
